What did the Token Diet Ledger measurements show?

One remote session, 4,231 API calls over 524 turns, read from its own transcript. Median context per call 442 K tokens, p90 695 K; 74% of calls ran with more than 300 K in the window. Tool results totalled 7.5 MB and were re-read on every later call.

Published findings
One remote session, 4,231 API calls over 524 turns, read from its own transcript. Median context per call 442 K tokens, p90 695 K; 74% of calls ran with more than 300 K in the window. Tool results totalled 7.5 MB and were re-read on every later call.
So terse prose can move at most the 7%. Fewer tokens per call, fewer calls per turn and a smaller fixed prefix (about 50 K tokens per session start) move the rest. Independent benchmarks agree: JetBrains measured caveman at −8.5% output tokens (advertised −65%), rtk at +7.6% cost, and Max Taylor found "Be brief." ties caveman on 24 chat prompts.
Each arm adds one lever to the previous one. Scored on total_cost_usd, an opencode judge rubric for chat (required key points, forbidden claims), and the task gate for agentic cases. Ladder 1: 384 sessions, $34.16. Ladder 3 re-measured the add-ons on the shipped config: 77 sessions, $5.99.
Scripted chains replayed into one session with --resume, so context growth and compaction are real. Whole-conversation cost on the 30-turn mixed chain: baseline $0.899 → soft style $0.655 (🪨 fell to 8 of 23 turns on the second run) → prefix diet $0.664 → hard style + quiet hooks + prefix diet $0.612 (−32%, 🪨 on 30/30 turns). The soft style decays; the hard one does not.
7 tasks (4 in-repo, 3 thrifty greenfield specs), 4 arms, n=3, 84 cells, 0 errors. Cell = mean total_cost_usd / mean seconds / gate pass rate.
The whole task on deepseek-v4-flash passes every gate at 1/30 of Sonnet's cost and 10% less wall-clock in total. Haiku halves cost at equal gates. In-session dispatch is a wash on cost, 2.8× slower and drops 3 gates: the mechanics and the eagerness, not the cheap model, are the problem.
Arm T is the cheap-flow pipeline without a human: a Sonnet session writes contract.md and units.jsonl (plan only; the task gate is pinned as the last unit's gate), then cheap-flow work runs every unit on the opencode implementer agent. One task per size, n=1, against B1's arm A means.
Reading. The plan step costs about $0.26 of Sonnet whatever the task size ($0.257, $0.263, $0.260), and the implementation $0.004–$0.021 of opencode. The headless tree therefore loses on small (2.7×) and medium (1.5×) tasks and wins on the large one (−54%). The crossover sits where Sonnet alone would spend more than about $0.30. Below it, the whole task on opencode (arm W, $0.004–$0.026, 7/7 gates) or Sonnet alone is cheaper; the plan's adoption rule (≥40% saved on ≥5/7 tasks) is not met for small and medium work. n=1 per task.
Where the tree earns its keep is the interactive flow measured live on a real feature (section 6): Claude spends three short documents and one adjudication, and the implementation, research, review and documentation cost about $0.07 of opencode.
The L3 reminder stays the shipped nudge. No lever reached the 80% first-call target; the honest conclusion is that the cheap model is best reached by choosing it up front (/cheap, /cf-*, wf opencode batch) rather than by persuading a running Sonnet session.
